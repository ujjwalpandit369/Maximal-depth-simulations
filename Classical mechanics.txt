1. Interactive Projectile Motion Laboratory
Create an animation of a cannon firing a projectile onto a flat landscape. The goal is to visualize how initial conditions and environmental factors affect the trajectory.
Scene Description: A 2D side-view scene with a cannon on the left side at a variable height, a flat ground plane, and a clear sky. The projectile's path should be traced on the screen as it flies.
Interactive Elements:
Sliders:
Initial Velocity (v₀): A slider ranging from 10 m/s to 200 m/s. This controls the magnitude of the initial speed at which the projectile leaves the cannon. A higher value will result in a longer and higher trajectory.
Launch Angle (θ): A slider ranging from 0° to 90°. This controls the angle of the cannon barrel relative to the horizontal. Observe how 45° gives the maximum range in the absence of air resistance.
Gravitational Acceleration (g): A slider from 1 m/s² to 25 m/s². This allows you to simulate the projectile on different planets (e.g., Earth ~9.8, Mars ~3.7). Higher gravity will cause the projectile to fall faster.
Air Resistance (Drag Coefficient): A slider from 0 (vacuum) to 1.0. This controls the magnitude of the air drag force (proportional to velocity squared). Notice how it shortens the range and makes the trajectory asymmetric.
Cannon Height (y₀): A slider to adjust the initial height of the cannon above the ground level.
Buttons:
Fire: Launches a new projectile with the current settings.
Reset: Clears all projectile paths and resets the simulation.
Visual Guides:
Trajectory Trace: A dotted or solid line showing the path of each projectile fired.
Vector Arrows: Checkboxes to toggle the display of Velocity (green) and Acceleration (red) vectors on the projectile as it moves.
Metrics Display: A small box showing real-time data: Time of Flight, Maximum Height, and Range (horizontal distance traveled).
Put everything in a single standalone html file.
