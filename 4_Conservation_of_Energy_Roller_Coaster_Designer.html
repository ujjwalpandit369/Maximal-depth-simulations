Plain-Language Overview

Energy is never created or destroyed, it only changes form. This is the law of conservation of energy, one of the most fundamental principles in all of science. This interactive simulation lets you explore this law in a fun and intuitive way by designing your own roller coaster! You can click to create hills and valleys, then release a cart and watch it ride your creation. A dynamic bar chart shows the cart's energy transforming between potential energy (stored energy due to its height) and kinetic energy (energy of motion). You'll see that as the cart goes down a hill, potential energy turns into kinetic energy and it speeds up. As it goes up the next hill, that kinetic energy turns back into potential energy. With no friction, the total energy remains constant, but turn on friction, and you can see how energy is gradually lost from the system as heat.

Beginner's Guide: Building with Energy

Step 1: Design Your Track

The simulation starts in design mode. Click anywhere on the canvas to add control points for your roller coaster track. A smooth curve will automatically be drawn through the points. You can click and drag any point to change the shape of your track. The height of the first point is the most important, as it determines the total amount of energy the cart will start with.

Step 2: Two Kinds of Mechanical Energy

When the cart is moving on the track, it has two kinds of energy that make up its total mechanical energy:

  • Gravitational Potential Energy (PE): This is the energy the cart has stored because of its height. The higher it is, the more potential energy it has. PE = mgh.
  • Kinetic Energy (KE): This is the energy the cart has because of its motion. The faster it's moving, the more kinetic energy it has. KE = ½mv².

Step 3: The Law of Conservation of Energy

When you release the cart, it starts at the top of the first hill with maximum potential energy and zero kinetic energy. As it rolls down, it loses height, so its PE decreases. But that energy doesn't disappear! It transforms into KE, so the cart's speed increases. As it goes up the next hill, it slows down, transforming KE back into PE.
The law of conservation of energy states that, if there is no friction, the **Total Mechanical Energy (TME = PE + KE)** remains constant at all times. Watch the energy bar chart as the cart moves—the PE and KE bars will go up and down, but their combined total (TME) will stay the same.

Step 4: The Effect of Mass and Friction

Try changing the **Cart Mass** slider. You'll notice that, as long as friction is zero, the cart reaches the same heights and has the same speed at any given point on the track, regardless of its mass! This is because mass cancels out of the energy conservation equation.
Now, increase the **Friction/Drag** slider. This introduces a force that removes energy from the system, converting it into heat. Watch the energy bar chart now. The TME bar will slowly shrink, and a red "Energy Lost" bar will grow. The cart will no longer be able to reach the same heights on subsequent hills.

Step 5: The Impossible Hill

Try to design a track where the second hill is taller than the first. Release the cart. What happens? It will roll partway up the second hill and then roll back down. It can never reach a height greater than its starting height (without friction) because that would violate the conservation of energy—it would require creating energy out of nothing!

Core Theory: Energy Conservation

1. Gravitational Potential Energy (PE)

PE = mgh

Where m is mass, g is gravitational acceleration (9.8 m/s²), and h is the height above a reference level.

2. Kinetic Energy (KE)

KE = ½mv²

Where m is mass and v is speed.

3. Conservation of Mechanical Energy

In a system without non-conservative forces like friction, the total mechanical energy is conserved. The initial energy is determined by the starting height h₀ (assuming the cart starts from rest).

Etotal = PEinitial = mgh₀

At any other point on the track, the energy is distributed between potential and kinetic:

Etotal = mgh + ½mv²

By setting these equal, mgh₀ = mgh + ½mv², we can solve for the speed at any height:

v = √(2g(h₀ - h))

Notice that the mass m cancels out, meaning the speed at a given height is independent of the cart's mass.
