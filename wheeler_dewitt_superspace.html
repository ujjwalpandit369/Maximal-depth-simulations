The Arena of Quantum Gravity

In classical physics, we think of a particle moving through space over time. Its state is its position, and its path is a line. But what if we want to describe the state of an entire universe, especially in a quantum theory of gravity? The "thing" that evolves is not a particle, but the very geometry of space itself.

Step 1: What is Superspace?

The panel on the left represents Superspace. This is one of the most mind-bending concepts in physics. It is an infinite-dimensional "space of all possible spaces."

Every single point in Superspace does not represent a location; it represents an entire 3D geometry of a universe.

Our simulation shows a tiny 2D slice of this infinite landscape. The glowing dot represents the current state of our toy universe.

The Wheeler-DeWitt Equation

In the 1960s, physicists John Wheeler and Bryce DeWitt formulated an equation that attempts to describe quantum gravity. The Wheeler-DeWitt equation describes a "wavefunction of the universe," which is a wave that exists on Superspace. Its value at each point tells you the probability amplitude for the universe to *have* that particular 3-geometry.

This leads to profound philosophical problems. For instance, the equation has no time variable. In this view, the evolution of the universe—what we perceive as time—is just the correlation between the different geometric variables encoded in a timeless wavefunction on Superspace.
