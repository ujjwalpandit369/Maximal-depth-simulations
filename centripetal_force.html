Centripetal Force on a String

An interactive demonstration of circular motion and inertia.

Overview

An object moving in a circle is constantly changing direction, which means it is constantly accelerating, even if its speed is constant. According to Newton's Second Law, an acceleration requires a net force. The force that causes this circular acceleration is called the centripetal force, and it always points towards the center of the circle. In this simulation, a ball is whirled on a string. The tension in the string provides the centripetal force. Explore how mass, speed, and radius affect the required force, and see what happens to the ball when that force is suddenly removed.

The Physics of Circular Motion

Centripetal Force is Not a "New" Force

It's important to understand that centripetal force is not a fundamental force of nature like gravity or electromagnetism. It is simply the net force required to keep an object in circular motion. This force can be provided by many things: the tension in a string, the force of gravity holding a planet in orbit, or the friction between a car's tires and the road in a turn. In this simulation, the tension is the centripetal force.

The Governing Equation

The magnitude of the required centripetal force is given by:

Fc = m * a_c = m * ω² * r
  • Fc is the Centripetal Force in Newtons (N).
  • m is the mass of the object. A heavier object requires more force to turn.
  • ω (omega) is the angular velocity in radians per second. A faster spin requires exponentially more force.
  • r is the radius of the circle. A larger circle also requires more force for the same angular velocity.

What Happens When the String Breaks?

Click the "Break String" button to see Newton's First Law (the Law of Inertia) in action. At the moment the string breaks, the centripetal force (tension) instantly drops to zero. With no net force acting on it, the ball no longer accelerates. It continues to move in a straight line with whatever velocity it had at that instant, which is always tangent to the circle. It does not fly straight outwards from the center.
