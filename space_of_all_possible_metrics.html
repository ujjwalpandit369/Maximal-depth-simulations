Guide: Spacetime as a Dynamic Field

The Metric Tensor: A Dynamic Ruler

In classical physics, space and time form a fixed, unchanging background—a stage on which events unfold. In Einstein's General Relativity, this is no longer true. Spacetime itself is a dynamic object, a field that can be stretched, bent, and warped. The object that describes this geometry is the metric tensor, `g_μν`.

Think of the metric as a set of rules for measuring distance and time at every point. In flat space, this is just the Pythagorean theorem. In curved space, the metric itself changes from point to point. The grid in the simulation is a visual representation of the metric. Initially, it's a uniform grid representing flat spacetime. When you "paint" on it, you are directly changing the components of the metric, causing the grid to stretch and deform.

Curvature from the Metric

The core idea of General Relativity is that curvature of spacetime is what we perceive as gravity. But where does this curvature come from? It arises from the non-uniform stretching of the metric.

The simulation calculates the Ricci curvature (represented by the color overlay) from the metric grid you create. Notice that curvature only appears where the grid is changing non-linearly. A uniformly stretched grid is still flat! It is the *change in the change* of the metric that creates curvature.

  • Red regions: Positive curvature (like the surface of a sphere).
  • Blue regions: Negative curvature (like the surface of a saddle).
  • No color: Zero curvature (flat).
This simulation demonstrates one half of Einstein's theory: Metric → Curvature. The full theory completes the loop: the presence of mass and energy determines the metric.
