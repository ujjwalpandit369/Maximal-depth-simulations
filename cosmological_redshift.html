The Expanding Universe & Cosmological Redshift

A 1D simulation showing how the expansion of space stretches light.

1D Universe

Scale Factor a(t): 1.000 | Photon Wavelength λ: 10.0 | Redshift z: 0.000

Hubble's Law

Important Distinction: Cosmological redshift is not a Doppler effect. The galaxies are not flying through space away from each other. Space itself is growing, carrying the galaxies along with it. The photon's wavelength gets stretched by this expansion of space itself.

Beginner's Guide

1. The "Rubber Band" Universe

The top black box represents our simplified 1D universe. Think of it like a long, black rubber band. The blue dots are galaxies, which are just drawn onto the rubber band. In their own local space, they are stationary.

2. Let There Be Light

Press the "Emit Photon" button. A photon (a particle of light, shown as a yellow wave) will be emitted from the first galaxy, traveling towards the right. For now, the universe is not expanding, so the photon travels along with a constant wavelength.

3. The Expansion

Now, press Reset and then press "Start Expanding". You'll see the "Scale Factor" `a(t)` start to increase. This is a measure of how stretched our rubber band universe is. Notice that all the galaxies begin to move apart from each other. The further away a galaxy is, the faster it appears to recede. This is the expansion of space.

4. The Stretched Photon

With the universe already expanding, press "Emit Photon" again. Watch the photon closely as it travels. As it flies through the expanding space, its own wavelength is stretched along with the fabric of space! You can see the yellow waves getting longer and longer. The "Photon Wavelength" value confirms this. The "Redshift z" value tells you the total percentage the wavelength has stretched since it was emitted.

5. Hubble's Law

When the photon reaches another galaxy, a red dot will appear on the "Hubble's Law" graph. This graph plots the final redshift (`z`) of the photon versus the distance to the galaxy it reached. Let the simulation run for a while, letting photons reach all the other galaxies. You will see that the points form a straight line. This is Hubble's Law: the further away a galaxy is, the greater its redshift. This linear relationship was the first major piece of evidence for the expansion of the universe.

Core Theory

The key concept is the scale factor of the universe, `a(t)`, which describes how distances stretch over time.

Equation: Expansion of Spacetime

D(t) = a(t) * D₀
  • Plain English: The physical distance `D(t)` between two galaxies at any time `t` is the original, "comoving" distance `D₀` multiplied by the scale factor `a(t)`. In our simulation, `a(t)` increases linearly, which is a simplification of more complex cosmological models.

Equation: Cosmological Redshift

λ_observed / λ_emitted = a(t_observed) / a(t_emitted)
  • Plain English: The ratio of the observed wavelength to the emitted wavelength is exactly equal to the ratio of the universe's scale factor when the light was observed to the scale factor when it was emitted.
  • The Redshift parameter `z` is defined as `z = (λ_observed - λ_emitted) / λ_emitted`. By substituting the first equation into this definition, we get:
    • 1 + z = a(t_observed) / a(t_emitted)

    This is the fundamental relationship. As the photon travels, `a(t_observed)` gets larger, so `z` increases. This is what the simulation calculates and displays.
