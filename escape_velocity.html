Black Holes and Escape Velocity

An interactive simulation on the formation of a black hole.

Plain-Language Overview

If you throw a ball up, it falls back down. If you throw it harder, it goes higher before falling. If you could throw it hard enough—at the "escape velocity"—it would never fall back. A black hole is an object so dense that its escape velocity is greater than the speed of light, the ultimate speed limit. Since nothing can travel faster than light, nothing can escape, not even light itself. This simulation lets you design your own planet. By changing its mass and radius, you can see how the escape velocity changes. If you make the planet dense enough to cross its "Schwarzschild Radius," you will create a black hole.

Beginner's Guide

1. Escape Velocity

Escape velocity is the minimum speed needed for an object to "break free" from the gravitational pull of a massive body without any further propulsion. For Earth, this speed is about 11.2 km/s (or 40,270 km/h). In this simulation, a cannon fires a projectile upwards at a fixed speed (0.3c). Whether it escapes or not depends on the planet's properties, which you control.

2. Mass and Radius

The escape velocity depends on two things: the mass of the planet and its radius.

  • Use the Mass slider. As you increase the mass, the gravitational pull becomes stronger, and the calculated escape velocity increases.
  • Use the Radius slider. As you *decrease* the radius (making the planet denser), you are getting closer to its center of mass, so the gravitational pull at the surface is stronger. This also increases the escape velocity.

3. The Schwarzschild Radius

The simulation calculates a critical value called the Schwarzschild Radius. This is the radius that an object of a given mass must be compressed to in order to become a black hole. It is the radius at which the escape velocity becomes equal to the speed of light.

Try to create a black hole! Increase the mass and decrease the radius until the planet's radius is less than or equal to its Schwarzschild Radius. When this happens, the escape velocity will exceed `1.0 c`. The planet will turn black, and when you fire the projectile, you will see that it can no longer escape, no matter how fast it's going. The edge of the black sphere is the event horizon—the point of no return.

The Key Equations

1. Escape Velocity

The escape velocity ve is derived by setting the initial kinetic energy of the projectile equal to the gravitational potential energy it needs to overcome.

ve = √(2GM/R)

Where G is the gravitational constant, M is the mass of the planet, and R is its radius.

2. Schwarzschild Radius

To find the radius at which an object becomes a black hole, we set the escape velocity equal to the speed of light, c, and solve for R.

c = √(2GM/Rs)
c2 = 2GM/Rs

Rs = 2GM/c2

This is the Schwarzschild Radius. Any object whose entire mass is contained within this radius is a black hole.
