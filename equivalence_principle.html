The Equivalence Principle: Gravity vs. Acceleration

Einstein's "happiest thought" visualized in an elevator.

Plain-Language Overview

The Equivalence Principle is the core idea behind general relativity. It states that the effects of gravity are completely indistinguishable from the effects of acceleration. This simulation lets you test this inside a sealed elevator. You can switch between two scenarios: the elevator sitting on Earth under gravity, or accelerating in deep space. By dropping a ball or firing a laser, you can see that if the acceleration `a` equals the gravitational field `g`, the observer inside can perform no experiment to tell which situation they are in.

Beginner's Guide

1. The Two Scenarios

Switch between two situations: On Earth (Gravity), where the elevator is stationary in a gravitational field `g`, and In Space (Acceleration), where the elevator accelerates upwards at `a` in zero gravity. The key is to set `g = a` and compare.

2. Experiment 1: Dropping a Ball

Press "Drop Ball." In the Gravity scenario, the ball falls. In the Acceleration scenario, the ball floats, but the elevator floor rushes up to meet it. To an inside observer, the results are identical.

3. Experiment 2: Firing a Laser

Press "Fire Laser." In the Acceleration scenario, the light travels straight, but the elevator moves up during the light's transit time, making the beam appear to curve down. By the Equivalence Principle, gravity must also bend light by the same amount. This was a key prediction of General Relativity.

Core Theory

For a dropped ball, the time to hit the floor is t = √(2h/g) in the gravity case. In the acceleration case, the floor moves up a distance `h` in time t = √(2h/a). If `g=a`, the times are identical. For the laser, the downward deflection is d = ½a(L/c)². The Equivalence Principle implies gravity must bend light by the same amount.
