The Symplectic Camel

A simulation to demonstrate the principle of symplectic non-squeezing.

Overview

In classical mechanics, the evolution of a physical system in phase space is governed by a special set of rules, described by symplectic geometry. These rules lead to some surprising and non-intuitive consequences. This simulation demonstrates one of the most famous: Gromov's non-squeezing theorem, colloquially known as the "symplectic camel." The theorem states that you cannot use a physical (Hamiltonian) evolution to squeeze a ball of states in phase space through a "needle's eye" of smaller radius, even if the needle is infinitely long (has more volume). Try as you might to deform the shape, its shadow on any 2D plane can never be made smaller than the original.

Beginner's Guide to Squeezing Camels

Step 1: Phase Space and Area Preservation

The canvas shows a 2D "phase space" with position (q) on the horizontal axis and momentum (p) on the vertical axis. The orange circle represents a collection of possible initial states for a physical system. A key property of classical mechanics is that as the system evolves in time, this collection of states can stretch and deform, but its total area must be preserved. Transformations that preserve area are called "symplectic."

Step 2: The Challenge

Your goal is to get the orange shape (the "camel") to pass through the red "eye of the needle." The needle's eye is narrower than the camel's initial diameter. Can you do it?

Step 3: The Tools (Transformations)

You have several buttons that apply transformations to the shape:

  • Apply Shear: This is a symplectic transformation. It preserves the area of the shape. Notice how it stretches the circle into an ellipse. The shape gets taller as it gets thinner. Try to use the shear to make the shape thin enough to fit through the needle. You'll find that its width never gets smaller than its original diameter!
  • Apply Rotation: This is also a symplectic, area-preserving transformation. It just rotates the shape.
  • Apply (Non-Physical) Squeeze: This is a non-symplectic transformation. It does *not* preserve area. It simply shrinks the shape in all directions. Notice that this transformation easily allows the shape to pass through the needle's eye.

The Punchline: The Non-Squeezing Theorem

The fact that you can't get the shape through the needle using the "physical" shear transformation, but you *can* with the "non-physical" squeeze, is the heart of the theorem. It reveals a fundamental rigidity in physical dynamics. While volume (or area in 2D) is preserved, there's a deeper constraint: the area of the shape's "shadow" on any position-momentum plane cannot be reduced. You can't squeeze a camel through the eye of a needle if you're only allowed to use the rules of physics.
