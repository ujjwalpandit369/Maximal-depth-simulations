The Fundamental Group (π₁)

A "lasso game" to explore the fundamental group of a topological space.

Est. runtime: N/A | Recommended device: Desktop | Approx. size: 25 KB

Overview

The fundamental group is a powerful idea in topology for classifying shapes based on the kinds of "loops" they contain. This interactive simulation turns this abstract concept into a "lasso game." Your goal is to draw a closed loop, or lasso, starting and ending at the red base point. Then, you "pull the lasso tight" by shrinking it. If the loop can shrink all the way back to the base point, it's considered "trivial." But if your lasso gets caught on one of the holes in the space, it cannot shrink to a point, revealing a non-trivial feature of the shape's topology. This process of classifying loops is the core idea behind the fundamental group, π₁.

Beginner's Guide to a Lasso Game

Step 1: The Space and the Base Point

The black area is our "topological space." It contains two important features:

  • The red dot is the base point. Every loop you draw must start and end here.
  • The gray circles are holes or "punctures" in the space. You cannot pass through them.

Step 2: Drawing a Loop (The Lasso)

To draw a loop, simply click your mouse anywhere on the canvas, drag it to draw a path, and release. The simulation will automatically connect your start and end points to the red base point to form a closed loop.

Step 3: Shrinking the Loop

Once you have a loop, click the "Shrink Loop" button. This will start an animation that pulls every part of the loop towards the base point.

There are two possible outcomes:

  1. The Loop is "Trivial": If your loop did not enclose any holes, it will successfully shrink all the way down to the base point and disappear. In the language of topology, this loop is "homotopic to the identity." It doesn't represent a fundamental feature of the space.
  2. The Loop is "Non-Trivial": If your loop went around one or more holes, it will get "snagged" on the holes as it tries to shrink. It cannot shrink to a single point. This reveals something fundamental about the space: it has a hole!

Exploring the Fundamental Group

Try drawing different kinds of loops to discover the "elements" of this space's fundamental group:

  • A loop around the left hole is different from a loop around the right hole.
  • A loop that goes clockwise around a hole is different from one that goes counter-clockwise.
  • A loop that goes around a hole twice is different from one that goes around once.

All these distinct, non-shrinkable loops are the different "elements" of the fundamental group of this punctured plane.
