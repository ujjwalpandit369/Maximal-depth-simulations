Length Contraction Explorer

Visualizing the paradox of the fast-moving spaceship and the tunnel.

Plain-Language Overview

Special relativity predicts that an object moving at high speed appears shorter in its direction of motion to a stationary observer. This is length contraction. This simulation explores a classic paradox: a long spaceship flies towards a shorter tunnel. From the tunnel's perspective, the ship shrinks and can fit inside. But from the pilot's perspective, the tunnel shrinks, making it seem impossible for the ship to fit. Who is right? This simulation lets you see both viewpoints to understand the resolution.

Beginner's Guide

1. The Setup: A Paradox

We have a spaceship with a "proper length" (measured at rest) of 100 meters and a tunnel with a proper length of 80 meters. The ship is longer than the tunnel.

2. The View from the Tunnel

Select "View from Tunnel". You are stationary, watching the ship approach. As you increase its velocity, its length shrinks. At v=0.6c, the ship's apparent length becomes 100m / 1.25 = 80m. It fits! Press "Animate" to see the moment it's fully inside.

3. The View from the Spaceship

Select "View from Ship". Now the ship is at rest (100m long), and the tunnel rushes towards it. The tunnel's length contracts to 80m / 1.25 = 64m. Now it seems impossible for the ship to fit. Press "Animate" to see that it doesn't.

4. The Resolution: Relativity of Simultaneity

Both observers are right! The paradox dissolves because "fitting inside" requires the ship's front and back to be inside the tunnel *at the same time*. But simultaneity is relative. In the Tunnel's Frame, the events are simultaneous. In the Ship's Frame, they are not. The front of the ship leaves the tunnel before the back ever enters. The event log shows the different event timings in each frame.

Core Theory

The physics is described by the Lorentz transformations. The transformation for time is t' = γ(t - vx/c²). Two events that are simultaneous in one frame (same `t`) but at different locations (different `x`) will have different times in another frame. This is the relativity of simultaneity, which resolves the paradox.
