Guide: Rubber Sheet Geometry

What is Topology?

Topology is a branch of mathematics sometimes called "rubber sheet geometry." It studies the properties of shapes that are preserved under continuous deformations, like stretching or bending, but not tearing or gluing. From a topological point of view, a coffee mug and a donut are the same because they both have exactly one hole. The number of holes is a fundamental topological property.

The Fundamental Group: Classifying Loops

One way to probe the topology of a space is by studying the types of closed loops you can draw on it. The set of all different "classes" of loops forms a mathematical structure called the fundamental group.

  • Trivial Loops: In the simulation, try drawing a loop that doesn't enclose the hole. When you press "Deform," it will shrink smoothly to the base point. All such loops belong to the "trivial" or "identity" class.
  • Non-Trivial Loops: Now, draw a loop that goes around the hole. When you try to deform it, the loop gets "caught" on the hole and cannot shrink to a point. This loop belongs to a different class.
Two loops are in the same class if one can be continuously deformed into the other. A loop that goes around the hole twice is in yet another class, distinct from the one that goes around only once. By classifying the loops, you are measuring the "holey-ness" of the space. A simple plane with no holes has only one class of loops (the trivial one). A plane with one hole has infinitely many classes, corresponding to how many times (and in which direction) a loop wraps around the hole.
