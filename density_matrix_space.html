Guide: From Pure States to Mixed Messes

The Bloch Sphere for Pure States

A qubit is the fundamental unit of quantum computing. A "pure" quantum state, where we have perfect knowledge of the system, can be visualized as a point on the surface of a sphere—the Bloch Sphere. The North Pole typically represents the "spin up" state, and the South Pole represents "spin down." Points on the equator represent equal superpositions of up and down.

Density Matrices and Mixed States

What if we don't have perfect knowledge? What if our qubit has a 50% chance of being spin up and a 50% chance of being spin down? This is not a superposition; it's a classical, statistical mixture. We describe such states using a density matrix (ρ). These mixed states live inside the Bloch sphere.

  • The Bloch Vector `r`: Any state, pure or mixed, can be described by a "Bloch vector" `r = (x, y, z)` that starts at the center of the sphere.
  • Pure States: If the state is pure, the vector's length `|r|` is exactly 1, and its tip touches the surface.
  • Mixed States: If the state is mixed, the vector is shorter (`|r| < 1`), and its tip is inside the sphere. The shorter the vector, the more "mixed" or "random" the state is.
  • Maximally Mixed State: The state at the very center (`r=0`) has zero length. This represents a completely random state (50/50 mixture of any two opposite pure states) and is called the maximally mixed state.

Purity: A Measure of "Quantumness"

The purity, `Tr(ρ²)`, is a mathematical way to quantify how mixed a state is. It ranges from 1 (for a pure state) down to 0.5 (for the maximally mixed state in a qubit system). Use the sliders to move the state vector around and observe how the length `|r|` and the purity change together. The space of all possible states for a qubit is not just the surface, but the entire solid Bloch ball.
