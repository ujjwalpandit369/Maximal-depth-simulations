Shadowing Lemma

A simulation to illustrate the concept of "shadowing" in chaotic systems.

Est. runtime: N/A | Recommended device: Desktop | Approx. size: 25 KB

Overview

Computer simulations of chaotic systems always have tiny numerical errors. Since chaotic systems are famously sensitive to initial conditions, does this mean our simulations are meaningless? The shadowing lemma provides a powerful and reassuring answer: no. It states that for many chaotic systems, any "pseudo-orbit"—a noisy trajectory that almost follows the system's rules, like a computer simulation—can be closely "shadowed" by a true, exact trajectory of the system. This simulation lets you explore this idea by drawing your own pseudo-orbit and then challenging the computer to find a true orbit that shadows it.

Beginner's Guide to Shadowing

Step 1: The Chaotic System

The canvas shows the phase space of the Hénon map, a classic chaotic system. The faint gray shape in the background is the Hénon strange attractor, the region where true trajectories of the system live.

Step 2: The Pseudo-Orbit (Your Drawing)

A "pseudo-orbit" is a path that doesn't perfectly follow the rules of the system. It could be the result of a noisy physical measurement or a computer simulation with floating-point errors. In this simulation, you create the pseudo-orbit yourself.

Click and drag your mouse across the canvas to draw a path. Try to draw a path that roughly follows the shape of the gray attractor. This orange path is your pseudo-orbit.

Step 3: Finding the Shadow

Once you've drawn a path, click the "Find Shadowing Trajectory" button. The simulation will then search for an initial condition for the Hénon map that generates a *true* trajectory which stays as close as possible to your hand-drawn path for its entire length.

After a moment, this true trajectory will be drawn in blue. You should see that even though your hand-drawn path was noisy and imperfect, there exists a real, perfect trajectory that "shadows" it very closely. This is the shadowing lemma in action! It gives us confidence that even though our computer simulations are imperfect, they are still representative of the true dynamics of the system.

Controls

1. Draw a "noisy" path on the canvas that stays close to the gray attractor.

2. Click the button to find a true trajectory that "shadows" your path.
