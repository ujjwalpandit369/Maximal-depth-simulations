Virtual Geiger Counter

An interactive simulation of a Geiger-Müller tube detecting radiation.

Overview

A Geiger counter is an instrument used for detecting and measuring ionizing radiation. Its heart is the Geiger-Müller tube, a sealed cylinder filled with an inert gas. When a single particle of radiation (like an alpha particle, beta particle, or gamma ray) passes through the tube, it ionizes a gas atom. A high voltage in the tube creates a strong electric field, which accelerates the freed electron. This electron then collides with other atoms, freeing more electrons in a chain reaction called a Townsend avalanche. This creates a large pulse of current that is registered as the iconic "click" of the counter. This simulation visualizes this entire process.
