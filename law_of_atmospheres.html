The Law of Atmospheres

A simulation of a gas column in a gravitational field.

Overview

Why is the air thinner at the top of a mountain? The answer is a competition between gravity and thermal energy. Gravity pulls air molecules down, while their own thermal motion (their temperature) causes them to spread out and fill their container. The Law of Atmospheres, or barometric formula, describes the equilibrium state of this competition. It shows that the density and pressure of a gas in a gravitational field are not uniform, but decrease exponentially with height. This simulation visualizes this effect with a column of gas particles and a corresponding graph of the density profile.

Gravity vs. Temperature

The exponential decay of atmospheric density is governed by the ratio of two energies: the gravitational potential energy needed to lift a particle, and the thermal energy the particle possesses.

ρ(h) = ρ₀ * e^(-mgh / kT)
  • ρ(h) is the density at height h.
  • ρ₀ is the density at the bottom (h=0).
  • mgh is the gravitational potential energy. A stronger gravity (g) or a heavier particle (m) makes this term larger, causing the density to fall off more quickly.
  • kT is the characteristic thermal energy. A higher temperature (T) gives particles more energy to fight against gravity, causing them to spread out more and the density to fall off more slowly.

The Scale Height

The term H = kT / mg is called the scale height. It's the characteristic distance over which the pressure or density decreases by a factor of e (about 2.718). A large scale height (high T, low g/m) means a thick, extended atmosphere. A small scale height (low T, high g/m) means a thin, compressed atmosphere that hugs the ground.

Try this: Set the temperature to its maximum and gravity/mass to their minimums. You'll see a large scale height, and the particles will be spread almost evenly. Now do the opposite: set temperature to its minimum and gravity/mass to their maximums. The scale height will be very small, and almost all the particles will be pinned to the bottom of the column.
