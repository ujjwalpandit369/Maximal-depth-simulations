The Belousov-Zhabotinsky (BZ) Reaction

A simplified cellular automaton model of an oscillating chemical reaction.

Est. runtime: Continuous | Recommended device: Desktop | Approx. size: 22 KB

Overview

Most chemical reactions proceed in one direction until they run out of reactants. The Belousov-Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction is a famous exception. It's a "chemical oscillator," a complex mix of chemicals that can produce stunning, dynamic patterns all on its own. This simulation uses a cellular automaton—a grid of cells following simple local rules—to model the BZ reaction's behavior. By "poking" the chemical medium, you can initiate waves of activity that propagate, interact, and form beautiful concentric circles and rotating spirals. This is a classic example of a non-equilibrium system where simple rules lead to complex, emergent behavior.

Beginner's Guide to Chemical Waves

Step 1: A "Petri Dish" of Cells

The canvas represents a petri dish filled with a chemical medium. We simulate this as a grid of "cells." Each cell can be in one of many states, which we group into three categories:

  • Resting (Black): The cell is quiescent, waiting for a trigger.
  • Excited (Bright Colors): The cell has been activated and is now "firing." It will spread its excitement to its neighbors.
  • Refractory (Fading Colors): After firing, the cell enters a "tired" or refractory state. During this time, it cannot be excited again. This period is crucial for creating the trailing edge of the waves.

Step 2: Simple Rules, Complex Patterns

The entire simulation runs on a simple set of rules that each cell follows at every time step:

  1. If a cell is **excited** or **refractory**, it simply becomes a little less so in the next step (its state value decreases by one).
  2. If a cell is **resting**, it looks at its neighbors. If enough of its neighbors are excited, the cell itself becomes excited in the next step.

That's it! These simple, local rules are enough to generate the complex, large-scale spiral and circular waves you see. The waves are a form of "emergent behavior."

Step 3: How to Use the Simulation

  1. Start the Simulation: Click the "Play/Pause" button to start the evolution.
  2. "Poke" the Medium: Click anywhere on the canvas to set a small patch of cells to the "excited" state. This will trigger a new wave. Clicking on the tail of an existing wave can break it and create a spiral.
  3. Reset: The "Reset" button clears the grid or can be used to set up different initial conditions, like a line of excited cells.

Controls

Click Play, then click on the canvas to "poke" the medium and create waves.
