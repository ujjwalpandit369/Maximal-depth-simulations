The Homology of a Manifold

A simulation to find the "holes" of different dimensions in a topological space.

Overview

How do mathematicians rigorously describe the "shape" of an object? One of the most powerful tools is homology, a concept from algebraic topology that formalizes the notion of "holes." This simulation provides a visual introduction to homology by calculating the Betti numbers (which count the holes) for various 3D shapes. You can discover that a simple sphere has one "void" but no "tunnels," while a donut-shaped torus has two distinct "tunnels" and one "void." By visualizing these features, you can gain an intuitive understanding of how topologists classify and distinguish different kinds of spaces.

Beginner's Guide to Counting Holes

Homology is like a toolkit for counting different kinds of holes in a shape.

Step 1: What Kind of "Hole"?

The number of holes depends on what dimension you're talking about. Homology groups, denoted Hn, count the n-dimensional holes. The rank of these groups, called the Betti number bn, is what we'll focus on.

  • H₀ / b₀: Counts 0-dimensional holes, which are just separate, disconnected pieces. If you have one object, b₀=1. If you have two separate objects, b₀=2.
  • H₁ / b₁: Counts 1-dimensional holes. These are loops, tunnels, or "handles" that you could thread a string through. A sphere has no such tunnels (b₁=0). A torus (donut) has two: one through the center hole and one around the "body" of the donut.
  • H₂ / b₂: Counts 2-dimensional holes. These are enclosed voids or cavities. A sphere encloses a void (b₂=1). A torus also encloses a void (b₂=1). A flat disk does not (b₂=0).

Step 2: Exploring the Shapes

Use the buttons to select a shape and see its Betti numbers. Then, click the "Visualize" buttons to see what they mean.

  • Sphere: You'll find b₀=1, b₁=0, b₂=1. It's one piece, has no tunnels, but encloses one void.
  • Torus: You'll find b₀=1, b₁=2, b₂=1. It's one piece, has two distinct tunnels, and encloses one void. The simulation will draw these two loops for you.
  • Two Spheres: You'll find b₀=2, b₁=0, b₂=2. It's two separate pieces. Each piece has no tunnels but encloses its own void.

How to Use the Simulation

  1. Choose a Shape: Use the top buttons to select a manifold to inspect.
  2. Rotate the View: Click and drag on the canvas to rotate the 3D model.
  3. Visualize Homology: Click the "Visualize H₀/H₁/H₂" buttons. The simulation will display the Betti number for that dimension and run an animation to highlight the corresponding features on the shape.
