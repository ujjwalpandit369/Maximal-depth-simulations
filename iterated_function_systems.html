The Chaos Game

How can you create something as complex and beautiful as a fern frond from a few simple rules? By playing the "chaos game." This is the algorithm that powers this fractal generator. It works like this:

  1. Start with a random point on the screen.
  2. Randomly pick one of a few pre-defined mathematical rules (an "affine transformation").
  3. Apply that rule to your current point to get a new point.
  4. Draw a dot at the new point's location.
  5. Make the new point your current point, and go back to step 2.
When you click "Start," the simulation does this hundreds of thousands of times. It seems like it should just produce a random mess of dots, but miraculously, a highly structured, intricate shape emerges. This shape is the attractor of the system of rules (the Iterated Function System).

Exploring the Fractals

You can choose from a few classic examples:

  • Barnsley Fern: This famous fractal uses four transformations. One (used only 1% of the time) creates the main stem. Two others create the left and right leaflets. A final one creates the curled tip of the frond.
  • Sierpinski Triangle: This uses three simple rules. Each rule takes a point and moves it halfway towards one of the three vertices of a large triangle.
  • Heighway Dragon: This is built from two rules and creates a beautiful, recursive dragon-like curve.

Self-Similarity

The most famous property of fractals is self-similarity. Use your mouse wheel to zoom into the Barnsley Fern. As you get closer, you'll see that each large leaflet is made up of smaller copies of the entire fern. And each of those smaller leaflets is made of even smaller copies, and so on, forever! This infinite, repeating detail is a hallmark of fractal geometry. You can click and drag the mouse to pan around the zoomed-in view.
