Intermittency Route to Chaos

A simulation showing the intermittency route to chaos in the logistic map.

Overview

Chaos doesn't always appear suddenly. One of the ways a predictable system can become chaotic is through intermittency. This simulation explores this route to chaos using the famous logistic map. As a control parameter is moved past a critical point, the system's behavior changes dramatically. It no longer settles into a simple, periodic rhythm. Instead, it exhibits long periods of regular, predictable motion (called "laminar" phases) that are suddenly interrupted by short, irregular, chaotic bursts. By adjusting the parameter, you can see how these chaotic bursts become more frequent and last longer, eventually consuming the entire signal.

Beginner's Guide to Intermittent Chaos

Step 1: The Time Series Plot

The canvas shows a "time series" plot. The horizontal axis is time (or, more accurately, the iteration number `n`), and the vertical axis shows the value of the system's variable `x` at that time. We are using the logistic map, xn+1 = r * xn * (1 - xn), as our simple model system.

Step 2: The "Window" of Order

The logistic map is famous for its bifurcation diagram, which shows regions of order and chaos. Within the chaotic region, there are small "windows" of stability. We are looking at the largest of these, the "period-3 window," which occurs around `r = 3.828`.

  • Periodic Behavior: Use the "Periodic" preset or set the `r` slider just below the critical value (e.g., to 3.828). You will see the signal quickly settle into a stable pattern that repeats every three steps. This is a stable, predictable "period-3" cycle.

Step 3: The Onset of Intermittency

Now, use the "Intermittent" preset or move the slider just slightly above the critical value (e.g., to 3.829). The picture changes completely.

  • Laminar Phases: Notice the long stretches where the signal looks almost exactly like the old period-3 cycle. This is the regular, "laminar" phase.
  • Chaotic Bursts: These regular phases are suddenly interrupted by short, violent bursts where the behavior is unpredictable and chaotic.

This is intermittency. The system is trying to be periodic, but it's unstable. It gets knocked out of the periodic rhythm into a chaotic burst, wanders around for a while, and then gets drawn back into the near-periodic behavior, starting the cycle over again. As you increase `r` further, you will notice the chaotic bursts become more frequent and last longer.

Controls

Use the slider to change the logistic map's parameter `r`. The transition to intermittency occurs just above `r ≈ 3.8284`.
