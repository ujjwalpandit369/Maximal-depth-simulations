Gravitational Wave Visualizer

An animation showing how ripples in spacetime stretch and squeeze a grid of particles.

Plain-Language Overview

A gravitational wave is a ripple spreading across spacetime itself, created by cosmic events like colliding black holes. These ripples don't move things *through* space; they stretch and squeeze space itself. This simulation shows a grid of free-floating particles. As a gravitational wave passes through, you can see how the distances between the particles change, revealing the invisible distortion of spacetime.

Beginner's Guide

1. Making Waves

Press Play to start the gravitational wave. You'll see the particles start to move as space is rhythmically stretched and squeezed. The Amplitude slider controls the wave's strength. The Frequency slider controls how fast the particles oscillate.

2. Plus (+) vs. Cross (×) Polarization

Gravitational waves have different orientations. In Plus (+) polarization, the central circle of particles is squeezed vertically while stretched horizontally, and vice-versa. In Cross (×) polarization, the distortion is rotated by 45 degrees, squeezing along the diagonals.

Core Theory

For a wave traveling out of the screen, the displacement of a particle `(Δx, Δy)` from its original position `(x, y)` is given by: Plus (+): Δx(t) = h*x*cos(ωt), Δy(t) = -h*y*cos(ωt). Cross (×): Δx(t) = h*y*cos(ωt), Δy(t) = h*x*cos(ωt). The amplitude `h` is the strain, and `ω` is the angular frequency (`2πf`).
