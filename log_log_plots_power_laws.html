Guide: The Power of Log-Log Plots

What is a Power Law?

A power law is a relationship between two quantities where one is proportional to a fixed power of the other. The general form is:

y = k * xᵃ

Here, 'a' is the exponent and 'k' is a constant of proportionality. These relationships are everywhere in science and nature:

  • The area of a square vs. its side length: Area = s² (a = 2, k = 1)
  • The force of gravity vs. distance: F = G(m₁m₂)/r² = k * r⁻² (a = -2)
  • Kepler's Third Law of planetary motion: T² ∝ R³ or T ∝ R¹·⁵ (a = 1.5)
On a standard linear plot, these relationships appear as curves, making it difficult to determine the exponent 'a' just by looking.

The "Magic" of Logarithms

Logarithms have a wonderful property: they turn multiplication into addition and powers into multiplication. Let's take the base-10 logarithm of both sides of the power-law equation:

log(y) = log(k * xᵃ)

Using the rules of logarithms, this becomes:

log(y) = log(k) + log(xᵃ)

And finally:

log(y) = a * log(x) + log(k)

This is the equation of a straight line, Y = mX + c, where:

  • Y = log(y)
  • m = a (the slope is the exponent!)
  • X = log(x)
  • c = log(k) (the y-intercept is the log of the constant)
So, if a set of data follows a power law, plotting it on a log-log scale will reveal a straight line. The slope of that line gives you the exponent 'a' directly. This is one of the most powerful tools for data analysis in science.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Load Data: Use one of the sample data buttons or enter your own 'x,y' data in the text box.
  2. Plot: Click "Plot Data". You will see the data on a standard linear scale. Notice it forms a curve.
  3. Switch Scale: Click "Switch to Log-Log Plot". The axes will change to a logarithmic scale (10⁰, 10¹, 10², etc.).
  4. Analyze: Observe how the curved data has transformed into a straight line. The tool automatically fits a line to this data and calculates the slope, which is the power-law exponent 'a'. The analysis appears below the plot.
