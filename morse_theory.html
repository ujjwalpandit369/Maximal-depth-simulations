How Mathematicians Build Shapes

Morse Theory is a beautiful branch of mathematics that connects the shape (topology) of a surface to special points on it called "critical points." It gives us a recipe for building complex shapes from simple pieces.

Step 1: The Landscape

The simulation shows a 2D surface—in this case, a torus (donut) standing on its side. We've defined a "height function" on this surface, which is just the vertical `z` coordinate. The critical points of this function are marked:

  • ● Minimum: The very bottom point.
  • ● Saddle Points: Two "pass" points on the sides.
  • ● Maximum: The very top point.

Step 2: The Sweeping Water Level

Use the "Sweep Height" slider to control the position of the semi-transparent plane. Think of this as a rising water level. The bright yellow line on the surface is the "waterline"—the intersection of the water level with the surface. This is called a level set.

Step 3: Watching Topology Change

The key idea of Morse Theory is that the topology (the number of pieces, the number of holes) of the surface below the water level only changes when the level passes a critical point.

Slowly move the slider from bottom to top and watch the info panel:

  1. At the MINIMUM: A new component is born. The waterline starts as a single point, then becomes a small circle. We have "created" a piece of the surface.
  2. Between the minimum and the saddles: As you raise the level, the waterline is just a single circle that gets bigger. Nothing topologically interesting happens.
  3. At the SADDLE POINTS: The magic happens here. The single circular waterline pinches in the middle and transforms into two separate circles. This is a fundamental change in topology, like adding a "handle" to our shape.
  4. Between the saddles and the maximum: Now we have two separate circles for our waterline, and they just shrink. No topological change.
  5. At the MAXIMUM: The two circles shrink down to a single point and disappear. We have "filled in" the final cap of our shape.
By understanding what happens at these four critical points, we have completely described how to build a torus: start with a disk (minimum), stretch it and glue its sides together (the two saddles), and then put a cap on the remaining hole (maximum).
