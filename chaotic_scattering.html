Guide: The Unpredictable Bounce

The Experiment

This simulation models a simple physics experiment: firing a particle at a set of three repulsive "hills." The particle is always fired from the bottom edge with the same initial energy. The only thing we change is its initial horizontal position, the "impact parameter." The question is: how does the final angle at which the particle exits depend on this impact parameter?

A Fractal Relationship

One might expect a smooth relationship—a small change in the starting position should lead to a small change in the final angle. But for this system, that is not the case. Press "Generate Plot" to see the result. The graph of Final Angle vs. Impact Parameter is not a smooth curve. It is a fractal, full of abrupt jumps and infinitely nested regions of complexity. This means that there are regions where a tiny, almost immeasurable change in the initial impact parameter leads to a wildly different outcome. This is a classic example of transient chaos.

Once the plot is generated, you can click on it. This will run a single trajectory simulation in the left-hand window for the impact parameter you selected, allowing you to see the physical path that corresponds to a point on the fractal graph.
