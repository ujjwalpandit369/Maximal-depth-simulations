Phase Diagram Space: The Ternary Plot

An interactive visualization of a three-component phase diagram.

Plain-Language Overview

A ternary plot is a special triangular map that shows the state, or "phase," of a mixture of three components at a fixed temperature and pressure. Each corner represents 100% of one component. Any point inside represents a unique blend. This simulation lets you explore such a map. You can click anywhere on the triangle to see the composition of your mixture and discover its phase—whether it's a uniform liquid, a solid, or a slushy mix.

Beginner's Guide

1. How to Read the Triangle

The equilateral triangle shows three percentages that must add to 100%. The corners are pure components (A, B, C). The edges are two-component mixtures. Any point inside is a mix of all three. Click to see the exact percentages.

2. What are "Phases"?

A phase is a uniform state of matter (e.g., liquid, solid). Our diagram shows regions for Liquid (L), Solid A (Sₐ), Solid B (Sₑ), and Solid C (Sₒ). In regions like "L + Sₐ", the mixture is a slushy of liquid and solid crystals of A. The central "Sₐ + Sₑ + Sₒ" region is fully solid.

3. Simulating Cooling

The "Simulate Cooling" button shows what happens as the mixture cools. Starting from a liquid, it will eventually hit a boundary where solid crystals start to form. This changes the composition of the remaining liquid, which follows a path on the diagram until it fully solidifies, often at the special "eutectic point" (E).

Core Theory

1. Barycentric Coordinates

A point P inside the triangle is represented by unique coordinates (a, b, c) where a + b + c = 1. These are its barycentric coordinates, corresponding to the fractions of components A, B, and C. The position is a weighted average of the corners: P = a*A + b*B + c*C.

2. The Lever Rule

In a two-phase region like L + Sₐ, a "tie line" connects the pure solid A to the liquid L it's in equilibrium with. The amounts of each phase are given by the lever rule: Fraction of Solid A = length(M-L) / length(A-L), where M is the point for the overall mixture. This is like a seesaw, where the "heavier" phase is closer to the fulcrum (M).
