Negative Absolute Temperature

An interactive demonstration of a system "hotter than infinity."

Overview

Can something be colder than absolute zero (0 Kelvin)? The answer is no. But can something have a negative absolute temperature? Surprisingly, yes. Negative temperature is a real, experimentally verified concept that occurs in special physical systems, like a collection of nuclear spins in a magnetic field. This simulation demonstrates how such a state is created. Counter-intuitively, a system at negative temperature is not cold at all; it is infinitely hot, and will give up heat to any system at any positive temperature.

How Negative Temperature Works

1. A Special Kind of System

Normally, the energy you can add to a system is unlimited. You can always make particles move faster. However, some quantum systems have a maximum possible energy state. The classic example, shown here, is a set of nuclear spins in a magnetic field. Each spin can either be low-energy (aligned with the field) or high-energy (anti-aligned with the field). The system's energy is highest when all spins are anti-aligned. There's no way to add more energy after that point.

2. Temperature and Population

Temperature is a measure of how energy is distributed among the available states. For any positive temperature (T > 0), from room temperature to the core of a star, there will always be more particles in low-energy states than in high-energy states. This is shown in the initial "Population vs. Energy" graph: the "Low Energy" bar is much taller than the "High Energy" bar.

3. Creating a Population Inversion

To achieve negative temperature, we need to flip this situation and create a population inversion, where more particles are in high-energy states than low-energy states. In the lab, this is done by applying a precisely timed radio-frequency pulse (a "180° pulse") that causes all the spins to flip their orientation simultaneously.
Click the "Apply 180° Pulse" button to see this happen. Notice how all the spins flip upwards, and the population graph inverts. The system is now in a negative temperature state.

4. Hotter Than Infinity?

Why is this "hot"? Heat always flows from a hotter object to a colder one. If you put our negative-temperature spin system in contact with *any* system at a positive temperature (even a trillion degrees), heat will flow *from* the spins *to* the other system. The negative temperature system is so eager to give up its energy that it acts as the hotter object in any interaction. The scale of temperature looks like this:

0 K → +300 K → +∞ K → -∞ K → -300 K → -0 K

Passing through infinity is the only way to get from the positive to the negative temperature regime. Therefore, negative temperatures are not "below zero" but are on the other side of infinity.
