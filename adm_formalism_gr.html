Overview

How do physicists simulate black hole mergers or the evolution of the universe? They often use the ADM formalism, a powerful way of recasting Einstein's 4D theory of General Relativity into a "3+1" dimensional problem. This approach treats spacetime as a "stack" of 3D spatial slices evolving through time. This simulation provides a conceptual visualization of this idea. You can scrub through time to see how the geometry of a slice of space, represented by a grid, changes over time as a gravitational wave passes through. This "Hamiltonian" or "initial value" formulation is the foundation of modern numerical relativity.

Beginner's Guide to Slicing Spacetime

Step 1: Spacetime as a Movie

Einstein's theory describes a 4-dimensional "block" of spacetime. This is hard to think about. The ADM formalism (named after its authors Arnowitt, Deser, and Misner) provides a more intuitive picture by "slicing" the 4D block into a sequence of 3D spaces, like the individual frames of a movie. The canvas in the simulation shows one such 2D slice (representing 3D space) at a specific moment in time.

Step 2: The State of Space

In this view, the "state" of the universe at a given time `t` is the complete geometry of that time's spatial slice. This geometry is defined by two pieces of data:

  • The Metric: This tells you how to measure distances within the slice. It defines the slice's intrinsic geometry. We visualize this as the shape of the grid itself.
  • The Extrinsic Curvature: This tells you how the slice is curved and embedded in the larger 4D spacetime. Crucially, it contains information about the "velocity" of the geometry—how it's changing in time.
The phase space of General Relativity is the space of all possible metrics and extrinsic curvatures.

Step 3: The Rules of Evolution (Constraints)

The geometry of a slice can't just evolve in any random way. The evolution from one slice to the next is strictly governed by Einstein's equations. In the ADM formalism, these equations take the form of constraint equations.

  • Hamiltonian Constraint: Relates the curvature of the slice to the energy density on it.
  • Momentum Constraint: Relates the change in geometry to the flow of momentum.

You can think of these as the "rules of physics" that ensure the stack of 3D slices glues together perfectly to form a valid 4D spacetime. In our conceptual simulation, the readouts simply confirm that our pre-calculated evolution obeys these rules.

How to Use the Simulation

  1. Use the Time Slider: This is your only control. It allows you to move forward and backward through the "movie" of evolving spatial slices.
  2. Observe the Grid: As you move the slider, watch how the grid representing space distorts and ripples. This animation shows a simple gravitational wave propagating through the slice, stretching and squeezing the geometry of space itself as it passes.
