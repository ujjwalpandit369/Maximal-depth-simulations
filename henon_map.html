Guide: The Shape of Chaos

Attractors: Where Systems Settle

A dynamical system is any system that evolves over time according to a fixed rule. An attractor is a set of states that the system tends to evolve towards, regardless of where it starts. A swinging pendulum, for example, has a simple "point attractor"—it always settles to rest at the bottom. The predator-prey system we saw earlier has a "limit cycle" attractor—it settles into an endless loop.

Strange Attractors

The Hénon map produces a strange attractor. This is an attractor that has a fractal structure. No matter how closely you zoom in on a piece of the Hénon attractor, you will find more structure. It is made of infinitely many fine, parallel strands. Use the zoom tool (click and drag a rectangle) to see this for yourself.

This fractal nature is a hallmark of chaos. The system's trajectory is confined to the attractor, but its movement within the attractor is aperiodic and highly sensitive to initial conditions. The Hénon map generates this complexity through a simple process of repeatedly stretching and folding the space of points, much like kneading dough.
