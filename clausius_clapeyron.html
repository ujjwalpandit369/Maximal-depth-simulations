The Clausius-Clapeyron Relation

Exploring the boundary between liquid and vapor.

Overview

Why does water boil at a lower temperature at high altitudes? The answer lies in the relationship between pressure, temperature, and phase transitions. The Clausius-Clapeyron relation is a fundamental equation in thermodynamics that describes the line on a pressure-temperature (P-T) diagram that separates two phases, such as liquid and vapor. It states that the slope of this line is not arbitrary, but is directly related to the latent heat of the transition, the temperature, and the change in volume between the phases. This simulation visualizes this relationship for the liquid-vapor transition of water.

The Coexistence Curve

The line on the P-T diagram is the liquid-vapor coexistence curve. At any point on this line, liquid and vapor can exist together in stable equilibrium. If you move off the line, only one phase can exist. As you adjust the temperature slider, the simulation automatically calculates the corresponding pressure required to stay on this line, using an integrated form of the Clausius-Clapeyron equation.

The Clausius-Clapeyron Relation

The core of the theorem is about the slope of this line:

dP/dT = L / (T * ΔV)
  • dP/dT is the slope of the P-T curve at a given point. The simulation shows this as a tangent line on the graph.
  • L is the Latent Heat of Vaporization: the energy needed to turn the liquid into a gas.
  • T is the absolute temperature at that point.
  • ΔV is the change in specific volume from liquid to gas (V_gas - V_liquid). Since the volume of gas is much larger than the liquid, this is approximately just V_gas.

The readouts below the simulation show the value of the slope measured directly from the curve and the value calculated from the right-hand side of the equation. You can see that they always match, providing an interactive proof of the relation!
