Dynamic Equilibrium: Evaporation & Condensation

Visualizing how vapor pressure is established in a sealed container.

Overview

In a sealed container partially filled with a liquid, a fascinating process occurs: molecules from the liquid are constantly escaping into the space above (evaporation), while molecules from the gas are simultaneously returning to the liquid (condensation). This simulation visualizes this process. Initially, evaporation happens faster than condensation, causing the number of gas particles—and thus the pressure—to rise. Eventually, the rates of evaporation and condensation become equal. At this point, the system has reached dynamic equilibrium, and the pressure of the gas stabilizes. This stable pressure is known as the vapor pressure. Use the slider to see how temperature affects this process.

Beginner's Guide

Imagine a dance floor (the liquid) next to a lounge area (the gas). Some dancers get energetic and jump into the lounge (evaporation), while some people in the lounge decide to join the dance (condensation). Increasing the temperature is like playing faster music—more dancers get energetic and jump into the lounge, increasing the "pressure" in that area.

Core Theory

This simulation illustrates a state of dynamic equilibrium where Rateevaporation ⇌ Ratecondensation. The rate of evaporation depends primarily on temperature. The rate of condensation depends on the concentration of gas particles. When these rates become equal, the pressure exerted by the gas phase is called the equilibrium vapor pressure. This pressure increases with temperature.
