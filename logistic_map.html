Order from Chaos

The logistic map is a simple mathematical formula that was originally intended to be a model of population growth. Despite its simplicity, it is one of the most famous examples of how chaotic behavior can arise from simple, non-linear equations.

Step 1: The Cobweb Plot

The left panel shows a "cobweb plot," which helps visualize the iteration.

  • The blue curve is the parabola `y = r·x·(1-x)`.
  • The gray line is `y = x`.
  • The red path shows what happens to our initial population `x₀`. To find the next value, we go up to the parabola, then horizontally over to the `y=x` line, then down. This new spot on the x-axis is our next value, and the process repeats.
Set the `r` slider to a low value, like 2.8. Notice how the cobweb quickly spirals into a single fixed point. The population becomes stable.

Step 2: The Bifurcation Diagram

The right panel shows the bifurcation diagram. This is the main event!
The horizontal axis is the growth rate `r` you are controlling with the slider. The vertical axis shows the long-term values that `x` eventually settles on for that value of `r`. The vertical yellow line shows the current `r` value you're inspecting with the cobweb plot.

Step 3: The Route to Chaos

Slowly drag the `r` slider from left to right and watch both plots.

  1. `r < 3.0`: The system settles to a single point. The bifurcation diagram shows a single line.
  2. `r ≈ 3.2`: The line splits in two! This is a period-doubling bifurcation. The population no longer settles to one value, but oscillates between two. You can see this 2-cycle clearly in the cobweb plot.
  3. `r ≈ 3.5`: The two lines split into four. The system now oscillates between four values.
  4. `r > 3.57`: This period-doubling cascade happens faster and faster until the system becomes chaotic. The points on the bifurcation diagram now fill entire bands, meaning the population never settles down and appears random (though it is still deterministic).
Look closely inside the chaotic region (e.g., around `r = 3.83`). You can see strange white "windows" where the chaos suddenly disappears and is replaced by stable cycles (like a 3-cycle). This pattern of order emerging from chaos, and chaos from order, is a deep and beautiful feature of non-linear systems.
