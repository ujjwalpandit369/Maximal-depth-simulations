The Relativity of Simultaneity

The train and lightning thought experiment.

Plain-Language Overview

We usually think "simultaneous" is absolute: either two things happen at the same time, or they don't. Einstein's special relativity reveals the answer depends on who you ask! This simulation shows his famous thought experiment: observers on a train and a platform watch two lightning strikes. From the platform, the strikes look simultaneous. But because the person on the train is moving, they will see the light from the front strike first and conclude the events were not simultaneous. This simulation lets you see the experiment unfold from both points of view.

Beginner's Guide

1. The Setup

An observer (T) is in the middle of a train, and another observer (P) is on the platform. Two lightning bolts strike the very front and back of the train car.

2. The View from the Platform

Select "View from Platform". When you run the experiment, two lightning bolts strike simultaneously. Light from each strike travels inwards. Since Observer P is in the middle, the light pulses reach them at the same time. P concludes the strikes were simultaneous.

3. The View from the Train

Select "View from Train". From T's perspective, they are at rest. They observe the front light flash arriving first. Because the speed of light is constant, T must conclude the front lightning strike actually happened earlier than the back one. Run the animation in this frame to see this happen explicitly.

4. The Conclusion: Simultaneity is Relative

There is no universal "now." Whether two events are simultaneous depends on your state of motion. Both observers' conclusions are correct within their own frame of reference.

Core Theory: The Lorentz Transformation for Time

The relativity of simultaneity is a direct consequence of the Lorentz transformation for time: t' = γ(t - vx/c²). In the platform frame, the two strikes happen at the same time `t` but at different locations `x₁` and `x₂`. Because `x₁ ≠ x₂`, their times in the train frame, `t'₁` and `t'₂`, will be different. The `vx/c²` term is the mathematical source of this relativity.
