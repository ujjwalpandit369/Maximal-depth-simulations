Guide: From Position to Jerk

The Language of Motion Graphs

The graphs above tell a complete story about an object's motion.

  • Position (x): Where the object is. This is the graph you draw.
  • Velocity (v): The slope of the position graph (dx/dt). It's how fast the position is changing. A steep upward slope is high positive velocity; a steep downward slope is high negative velocity.
  • Acceleration (a): The slope of the velocity graph (dv/dt or d²x/dt²). It's how fast the velocity is changing.
  • Jerk (j): The slope of the acceleration graph (da/dt or d³x/dt³). It's how fast the acceleration is changing. A high jerk feels like a sudden "jolt" in a car.

Curvature and Acceleration

While velocity is the slope of the x-t graph, acceleration is its curvature.

  • When the x-t graph is curving upwards (like a smile ◡), the acceleration is positive.
  • When the x-t graph is curving downwards (like a frown ◠), the acceleration is negative.
An inflection point is where the curve changes from smiling to frowning (or vice-versa). At this exact moment, the curve is momentarily straight, meaning its curvature is zero. Look at the graphs: you'll see that wherever the top graph has an inflection point, the acceleration graph crosses zero.
