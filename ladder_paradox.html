The Ladder in the Barn Paradox

An animation resolving a classic relativity puzzle with the relativity of simultaneity.

The Paradox

A ladder (10m long) runs towards a barn (8m long). From the barn's view, the ladder shrinks and fits inside. From the ladder's view, the barn shrinks, so it can't possibly fit. Who is right? This simulation shows that both are, because they disagree on whether the barn doors close "at the same time."

Beginner's Guide

1. The Barn's Point of View

Select "Barn" view. At high speed (e.g., 0.8c), the ladder contracts and appears shorter than the barn. Press "Play". The event log will show both doors closing simultaneously, trapping the ladder for an instant.

2. The Ladder's Point of View

Select "Ladder" view. The barn now appears contracted. Press "Play". The event log shows the resolution: the back door closes and opens *first*, letting the back of the ladder pass through. Only later does the front door close and open. The events are not simultaneous, so the ladder is never fully trapped.

Core Theory: Relativity of Simultaneity

The time `t'` of an event in a moving frame is related to the time `t` and position `x` in the rest frame by the Lorentz Transformation: t' = γ(t - vx/c²). Two events that are simultaneous in one frame (same `t`) but at different locations (different `x`) will have different times in another frame. This resolves the paradox.
