Wave Propagation in a 2T Universe

A demonstration of causality in a universe with two time dimensions.

Overview

In our universe, cause and effect are strictly ordered by the passage of time. An event here can only affect things in its "future light cone." This orderly progression is a direct consequence of the mathematical form of the wave equation in a universe with one time dimension (a "hyperbolic" equation). But what if there were two time dimensions? The wave equation would become "ultrahyperbolic," and as this simulation demonstrates, the neat and tidy structure of causality would completely break down. A single event would no longer be localized, but would have instantaneous, global consequences, making a predictable physical reality impossible.

The Light Cone and Causality

In our universe, if you snap your fingers, the light and sound from that event travel outwards at a finite speed. On a spacetime diagram (where time is the vertical axis and space is the horizontal), the region of spacetime that can be affected by your snap forms a "V" shape pointing upwards. This is the future light cone. Similarly, the only events that could have caused your snap are in its past light cone (a "V" pointing downwards). Events outside these cones are causally disconnected from your snap. This structure is fundamental to the predictable nature of our universe.

The Ultrahyperbolic Breakdown

The mathematics governing waves is called a wave equation. In our (1+3)D spacetime, this equation is "hyperbolic," and its solutions naturally produce the light cone structure. However, if you add a second time dimension, the equation changes type and becomes "ultrahyperbolic."

An ultrahyperbolic equation has dramatically different properties. One of the most critical is that the "initial value problem" is no longer well-posed. This means you can't simply define the state of the universe at one moment in time and expect the equations to give you a unique, predictable future. Instead, the state of the universe at all times is linked together by a complex global constraint. A "ripple" at one point is not an independent event; it has implications for the entire history of the universe, past and future.

What You See in the Simulation

  • Hyperbolic (1T) Mode: When you click, you create a local event. A wave propagates outwards from that point in a V-shaped light cone, just as it should in our universe. Events outside the "V" are unaffected.
  • Ultrahyperbolic (2T) Mode: When you click, you are attempting to create a local event without satisfying the required global constraints of this strange universe. The result is a catastrophic breakdown. The "ripple" doesn't propagate locally; the entire spacetime reacts instantly, demonstrating that in such a universe, every point is inextricably linked to every other point in a way that defies our intuitive understanding of cause and effect.

FAQ & Troubleshooting

1. Why is this a problem for physics?
Because physics relies on predictability. If the state of the universe right now isn't enough to predict the state a moment later, the scientific method breaks down. The well-posed initial value problem is a cornerstone of physical theories. Its absence in a 2T universe is a primary reason why such theories are not widely considered viable models of our reality (without special modifications, like in Bars' 2T-physics).
2. Is this related to the other "Two Time" simulation?
Yes, they explore different facets of the same core idea. The other simulation ("A Universe with Two Species of Time") shows a specific model (Bars' 2T-Physics) that *rescues* the theory by introducing a special gauge symmetry to hide the problematic effects of the second time dimension. This simulation shows the *raw problem* that theories like Bars' are trying to solve: the fundamental breakdown of causality if you simply add a second time dimension without such a symmetry.
3. Could a universe like this actually exist?
Most physicists would say no, precisely because of the problems demonstrated here. A universe where every event instantly affects every other event in space and time would be incomprehensibly chaotic and would not allow for the stable, ordered structures (like galaxies, stars, and us) that we see.
