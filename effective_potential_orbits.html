Guide: The 1D Trick for a 2D Problem

The Challenge of Orbits

Analyzing the full 2D motion of a planet can be complex. It has both radial motion (moving closer to or farther from the star) and angular motion (moving around the star). However, physicists developed a clever mathematical trick to simplify this. By combining the gravitational potential energy with a term for the energy of angular motion, they created a single 1D effective potential that depends only on the radius, `r`.

Two Competing Forces

The effective potential, `U_eff(r)`, shown in the main graph, has two parts:

  1. The Gravitational Potential (`-1/r`): This is the familiar attractive potential that pulls the planet towards the star. It dominates at large distances.
  2. The Angular Momentum Barrier (`L²/2r²`): This term isn't a real "force" but represents the energy tied up in the planet's sideways motion. Because angular momentum (`L`) is conserved, a planet moving closer to its star (`r` decreases) must speed up its angular velocity to compensate. This requires a lot of kinetic energy, so it's as if there's a powerful repulsive barrier preventing the planet from getting too close to the star. This term dominates at small distances.
The sum of the attractive gravitational term and the repulsive angular momentum term creates the potential well you see in the graph.

Reading the Graph

  • Energy Line (Green): When you click on the graph, you set the planet's total energy, `E`. Since energy is conserved, the planet is free to move horizontally on this graph.
  • Turning Points: A planet cannot enter a region where its total energy `E` is less than the potential energy `U_eff`. Thus, the planet is trapped in the potential well, oscillating between the two points where the green energy line intersects the pink potential curve. These two points are the perihelion (closest approach) and aphelion (farthest distance) of the orbit.
  • Circular Orbits: If you set the energy to the exact minimum of the potential well, the perihelion and aphelion are the same. The planet is stuck at a single radius, resulting in a perfect circular orbit.
  • Unbound Orbits: If you set the energy to a positive value (`E > 0`), the planet is no longer trapped in the well. It has enough energy to escape the star's gravity, resulting in a hyperbolic (unbound) orbit.
