Guide: From Stable Cycles to Chaos

Revisiting the Predator-Prey Model

The standard Lotka-Volterra model produces stable, predictable oscillations—a single closed loop in the phase space. This is a good first approximation, but real ecosystems are rarely so simple. They are affected by external factors, such as seasonal changes in weather and food availability.

Adding a "Season"

This simulation adds a "seasonal forcing" term to the prey's birth rate. Think of it as the grass for the rabbits being more plentiful in the "summer" and scarcer in the "winter." This periodic change is controlled by the slider.

  • A = 0: With zero forcing, you see the classic, stable Lotka-Volterra cycle. The system is perfectly periodic.
  • Small A: As you add a small amount of seasonal forcing, the single loop splits into two, then four, and so on. This is the same period-doubling cascade that signals the onset of chaos in many systems. The population no longer repeats every cycle, but every 2nd or 4th cycle.
  • Large A: With strong seasonal forcing, the system becomes fully chaotic. The trajectory never exactly repeats, and it traces out a complex, fuzzy shape. This is a strange attractor. The populations still rise and fall, but their peaks are no longer predictable. This demonstrates how a simple, periodic system can become chaotic when driven by an external periodic force.
