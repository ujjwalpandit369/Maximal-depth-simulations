Biophysics: Protein Folding

A simplified 2D lattice model (HP Model) of hydrophobic collapse.

Overview

How does a long, floppy chain of amino acids spontaneously fold itself into a precise, functional 3D shape? This is the protein folding problem, one of the biggest challenges in biophysics. A primary driving force is the hydrophobic effect. Some parts of the protein chain are "hydrophobic" (water-hating), while others are "hydrophilic" (water-loving). To minimize its energy, the chain contorts itself to hide the hydrophobic parts in a compact core, away from the surrounding water. This simulation uses a simplified HP (Hydrophobic-Polar) model on a 2D lattice to demonstrate this fundamental principle of self-assembly.
