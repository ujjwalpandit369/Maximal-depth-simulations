Guide: A Hairy Ball of Circles

What is a Fibre Bundle?

A fibre bundle is a mathematical object that is locally "a product of two spaces," but may have a different global structure. A simple analogy is a hairbrush: the handle can be considered the "base space." At every point on the handle, a bristle sticks out—this is the "fibre." The entire hairbrush is the "total space."

The Hopf Fibration: A Special Kind of Bundle

The Hopf Fibration is one of the most beautiful and important examples of a fibre bundle. In this case:

  • The Total Space is the 3-sphere (S³), which is a sphere living in 4-dimensional space. We can't see it directly, so we view its projection into our 3D world.
  • The Base Space is the familiar 2-sphere (S²), like the surface of a globe. This is shown on the left.
  • The Fibre is a circle (S¹).
What this means is that the 3-sphere can be thought of as being "made of" circles. There is one unique circle fibre corresponding to every single point on the surface of the 2-sphere base.

How to Use the Simulation

Click on a point on the Base Space sphere (left). The simulation will then calculate and draw the corresponding circular fibre in the 3D view on the right. Notice how different points on the sphere give rise to different circles in space. The North and South poles correspond to special fibres (a circle of infinite radius, i.e., a straight line, and a unit circle around the origin). Press "Animate Fibration" to sweep through all the points on the base sphere and see how the circular fibres are all interlinked, filling up all of space and forming the 3-sphere.
