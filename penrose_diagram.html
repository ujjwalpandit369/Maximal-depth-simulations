Guide: Mapping Infinity to a Finite Diamond

The Problem with Infinity

Spacetime is infinite. How can you possibly draw a map of it? This is the problem Penrose diagrams solve. A Penrose diagram is a clever mathematical trick—a type of conformal transformation—that squashes an infinite spacetime into a finite diagram, much like a Mercator projection squashes the spherical Earth onto a finite flat map.

This transformation heavily distorts distances (especially near the edges), but it faithfully preserves something much more important for relativity: angles. By preserving angles, it ensures that the paths of light rays are always shown as straight lines at 45 degrees. This allows us to see the entire causal structure of the spacetime—what can affect what—at a single glance.

Anatomy of the Diagram

  • Vertical Direction: Represents time.
  • Horizontal Direction: Represents space.
  • Light Rays: Always travel at 45 degrees. The path of anything that has mass must be "more vertical" than 45 degrees.
  • ℐ⁺ (Future Null Infinity): "Scri-plus". This is where light rays end. It's the "destination" of all photons.
  • ℐ⁻ (Past Null Infinity): "Scri-minus". This is where light rays begin. It's the "origin" of all photons.
  • i⁺ (Future Timelike Infinity): The destination for any massive observer. All worldlines of objects with mass must end here.
  • i⁻ (Past Timelike Infinity): The origin for any massive observer. All their worldlines begin here.
  • i⁰ (Spacelike Infinity): A single point representing all of "very far away" in space.
