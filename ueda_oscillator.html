The Ueda Oscillator

A simulation of a chaotic electrical circuit.

Est. runtime: Continuous | Recommended device: Desktop | Approx. size: 25 KB

Overview

In 1961, a Japanese graduate student named Yoshisuke Ueda was studying non-linear electrical circuits using an analog computer. He discovered a system that produced bizarre, non-repeating, yet structured behavior that he called "randomly transitional phenomena." Unbeknownst to much of the Western world for years, he had discovered one of the first-ever examples of a strange attractor. This simulation implements the equation for Ueda's oscillator. By adjusting the parameters, you can explore the system's trajectory in phase space and see the formation of the beautiful and historically significant Ueda strange attractor.

Beginner's Guide to Ueda's Attractor

Step 1: The Circuit and Equation

The system Ueda studied was a simple series resonance circuit, but with a non-linear inductor, driven by an AC voltage source. Its behavior is described by the differential equation:

d²x/dt² + k(dx/dt) + x³ = B cos(t)

  • x represents the current flowing through the inductor.
  • dx/dt (the velocity in phase space) represents the voltage across the capacitor.
  • k is the damping or resistance in the circuit.
  • B is the amplitude of the driving AC voltage.

Step 2: The Phase Space Plot

The canvas shows the "phase space" of the oscillator. The horizontal axis is the current (x), and the vertical axis is the voltage (dx/dt). The complete state of the circuit at any moment is a single point on this graph. As the circuit evolves, this point traces a path. After a short initial time, this path settles onto a final shape called the system's "attractor."

Step 3: Finding the Strange Attractor

Unlike some systems that have simple, predictable attractors (like a single point or a closed loop), the Ueda oscillator, for certain parameters, has a strange attractor. This means its long-term behavior is:

  • Aperiodic: The trajectory never exactly repeats itself.
  • Deterministic: The path is not random; it follows the exact rules of the equation.
  • Fractal: The attractor has an infinitely detailed, folded structure.

Use the sliders or the "Load Classic Attractor" preset to explore the system. The simulation continuously traces the trajectory, slowly revealing the intricate shape of the Ueda attractor.
