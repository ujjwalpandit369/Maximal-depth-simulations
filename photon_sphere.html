The Photon Sphere: Orbits of Light

A visualization of the path of light near a black hole.

Plain-Language Overview

A black hole's gravity is so strong that it can trap light itself. The boundary of no return is the event horizon. But outside the event horizon, there is another special region called the photon sphere. At this precise distance, gravity is just strong enough to bend light into a circular orbit. These orbits are unstable—like balancing a pencil on its tip—so the slightest nudge will cause a photon to either fly away or spiral into the black hole. This simulation lets you fire photons at a black hole and try to find this elusive orbit by adjusting your aim.

Beginner's Guide

1. The Black Hole and its Regions

The simulation shows a top-down view of a non-rotating black hole.

  • The Event Horizon (Inner Circle): This is the point of no return. Anything, including light, that crosses this boundary can never escape.
  • The Photon Sphere (Outer Dashed Circle): This is located at 1.5 times the radius of the event horizon. Only at this specific distance can light enter a temporary, unstable circular orbit.

2. Firing Photons

You can fire photons from the left side of the screen. Use the "Aiming Offset" slider to control the initial vertical position of the photon. This is known as the "impact parameter."

  • Aim far from the black hole: If the offset is large, the photon will only be slightly deflected by the black hole's gravity and will continue on its way.
  • Aim too close to the black hole: If the offset is too small, the photon's path will be bent so severely that it will spiral inwards and be captured by the event horizon.
  • Aim for the Photon Sphere: If you adjust the slider to a very specific value (the "critical" impact parameter), you can get the photon to make one or more orbits at the photon sphere before it either escapes or falls in. Try to find this value! (Hint: It's around ±87 on the slider).

The "Fire Burst" button fires several photons at once with slightly different offsets, letting you see all three outcomes simultaneously.

Effective Potential and Unstable Orbits

In General Relativity, the path of a particle can be analyzed by looking at its "effective potential." For a massive particle orbiting a black hole, this potential has a "valley" which allows for stable orbits (like planets).

For a massless particle like a photon, the effective potential is different. It has no stable valley, but only a single peak located at r = 1.5 Rs, where Rs is the Schwarzschild radius (the radius of the event horizon). This peak corresponds to an unstable equilibrium. A photon with exactly the right energy and angular momentum can balance on this peak, resulting in a circular orbit. Any tiny perturbation will cause it to fall off the peak, either inwards towards the event horizon or outwards to infinity.
