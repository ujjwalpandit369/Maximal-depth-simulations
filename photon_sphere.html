The Photon Sphere: Orbits of Light

A visualization of the path of light near a black hole.

Plain-Language Overview

A black hole's gravity can trap light. The point of no return is the event horizon. But outside this, there's a special region called the photon sphere where gravity is just strong enough to bend light into a circular orbit. These orbits are unstable—like balancing a pencil on its tip. This simulation lets you fire photons at a black hole and try to find this elusive orbit by adjusting your aim.

Beginner's Guide

1. Firing Photons

Use the "Aiming Offset" slider to control the initial vertical position of the photon. Aim far, and the photon is only slightly deflected. Aim too close, and it gets captured by the event horizon. Aim at the critical value (around ±87), and you can trap the photon in an unstable orbit in the photon sphere (the dashed circle).

Core Theory

For a massless particle like a photon, the effective potential of a black hole has no stable "valley" for an orbit, only a single peak at r = 1.5 Rₛ (where `Rₛ` is the event horizon radius). This peak corresponds to an unstable equilibrium. A photon with exactly the right energy can balance on this peak in a circular orbit, but any perturbation will cause it to fall inwards or escape outwards.
