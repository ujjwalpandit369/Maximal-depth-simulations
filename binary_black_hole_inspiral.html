Binary Black Hole Inspiral

An animation of two orbiting black holes losing energy to gravitational waves, causing them to spiral inwards and merge.

Plain-Language Overview

As two black holes orbit, they create ripples in spacetime called gravitational waves. The energy for these waves is stolen from the orbit, causing the black holes to spiral closer and faster. This "inspiral" ends in a violent merger. This simulation shows this cosmic dance and the resulting "chirp" waveform that observatories like LIGO detect.

Beginner's Guide

1. The Inevitable Inspiral

The "Orbital View" shows two black holes. Press Play to see them orbit. They slowly get closer together because they are losing energy by emitting gravitational waves (faint purple ripples).

2. Listening to Spacetime: The "Chirp"

The "Gravitational Waveform" shows the signal a distant detector would receive. As the black holes get closer, the signal becomes taller (higher amplitude) and more compressed (higher frequency). This rising "chirp" is the signature of a black hole merger.

3. The Controls

You can change the Masses and Initial Separation of the black holes. More massive black holes and smaller separations lead to much faster mergers.

Core Theory

The simulation uses Post-Newtonian approximations. The power radiated as gravitational waves is given by P = - (32/5) * (G⁴/c⁵) * (m₁m₂)²(m₁+m₂) / r⁵. This energy is removed from the orbital energy, E = -G m₁m₂ / (2r), causing the separation `r` to decrease in a runaway feedback loop.
