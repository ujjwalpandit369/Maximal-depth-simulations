The Map

Arnold's Cat Map is a simple transformation that powerfully demonstrates chaotic behavior. It takes every pixel of the image and moves it to a new location on a "torus"—a square with wrap-around boundaries (like the screen in the classic game 'Asteroids'). The rule for moving a pixel from `(x, y)` to its new location `(x', y')` is simple:

x' = (2x + y) mod N
y' = (x + y) mod N

Where `N` is the width of the image.

Chaotic Mixing

Despite the simplicity of the rule, its effects are profound. After just a few iterations, the image is stretched, sheared, and folded until it becomes completely unrecognizable. Any two points that were initially close together are rapidly separated and end up in completely different parts of the image. This is a key feature of chaos known as sensitive dependence on initial conditions.

Poincaré Recurrence

Here is the magic. If you keep iterating the map, eventually the original image will reappear perfectly unharmed! This is a result of the Poincaré Recurrence Theorem. Since the map is just a permutation of a finite number of pixels, the sequence of states must eventually repeat. For the cat map, this recurrence time can be calculated and is often surprisingly short. For the `256x256` image used here, the image will return after 192 iterations.
