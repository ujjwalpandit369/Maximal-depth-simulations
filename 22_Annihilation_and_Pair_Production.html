Annihilation and Pair Production Simulator

Visualizing Einstein's E=mc² as energy turns into matter and back again.

Plain-Language Overview

Einstein's famous equation, E=mc², tells us that mass and energy are two sides of the same coin. Under the right, high-energy conditions, one can literally turn into the other. This simulation visualizes the two most famous examples of this process. In **Pair Production**, pure energy in the form of a high-energy photon transforms into a pair of matter and antimatter particles (an electron and a positron). In **Annihilation**, a particle and its antiparticle meet and vanish, converting their entire mass back into pure energy in the form of two photons. This interactive animation lets you trigger these events and see the fundamental dance of matter and energy unfold.

Beginner's Guide: The Dance of Matter and Energy

Step 1: What is Antimatter?

For every fundamental particle of matter, there exists an "antiparticle" with the same mass but opposite charge. The antiparticle of the negatively charged electron is the positively charged **positron (e⁺)**. When matter and antimatter meet, they annihilate each other in a flash of energy.

Step 2: Pair Production (Energy → Matter)

Click the **"Show Pair Production"** button to see this process.

  1. A high-energy photon (γ), which is a particle of pure light energy, travels through space.
  2. For energy to become matter, the photon must have at least enough energy to create the mass of the two particles. For an electron-positron pair, this threshold is 1.022 Mega-electron-volts (MeV). Use the slider to set the photon's energy.
  3. The photon must also pass close to a heavy atomic nucleus. The nucleus is needed to "absorb" some recoil to ensure that momentum is conserved, but it isn't otherwise changed.
  4. If the energy is high enough, the photon disappears, and an electron (e⁻) and a positron (e⁺) spring into existence and fly apart. Energy has become mass.
Try setting the energy below the threshold. You'll see the photon passes by without anything happening.

Step 3: Annihilation (Matter → Energy)

Click the **"Show Annihilation"** button to see the reverse process.

  1. An electron (e⁻) and a positron (e⁺) move towards each other.
  2. When they meet, they annihilate. Their mass, plus any kinetic energy they had, is converted back into pure energy.
  3. This energy is released in the form of two high-energy photons (γ) that fly away in opposite directions.

Step 4: Why Two Photons?

You might wonder why two photons are created in annihilation, not just one. This is because of the law of conservation of momentum. If the electron and positron collide head-on, the total initial momentum is zero. If only one photon were created, it would have to fly off in some direction, carrying momentum and violating the conservation law. By creating two photons that fly off in opposite directions, their momenta cancel out, and the total final momentum is also zero, satisfying the law.
