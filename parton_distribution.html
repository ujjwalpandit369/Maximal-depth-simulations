Parton Distribution Functions

A visualization of the dynamic structure of a proton.

Overview

What is a proton made of? The simple high school picture is three quarks (two "up" and one "down"). But the reality, as revealed by high-energy experiments, is far more complex. A proton is a roiling, dynamic sea of fundamental particles: the three "valence" quarks are swimming in a soup of constantly appearing and annihilating virtual "sea" quarks, antiquarks, and the gluons that bind them all together. Parton Distribution Functions (PDFs) are mathematical functions that describe the probability of finding a particular type of particle (a "parton") inside a proton carrying a certain fraction of the proton's total momentum. This simulation visualizes how our picture of the proton changes as we probe it at higher and higher energy scales.
