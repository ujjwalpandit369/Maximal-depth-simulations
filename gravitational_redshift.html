Gravitational Redshift & Blueshift

A simulation showing how light changes frequency as it travels through a gravitational field.

Plain-Language Overview

Gravity makes light lose energy when it tries to escape, and gain energy when it falls in. For light, a change in energy means a change in frequency and color. Light climbing out of a gravity well is "redshifted" (lower frequency), and light falling in is "blueshifted" (higher frequency). This simulation shows a photon traveling between a planet and a satellite, visualizing this effect.

Beginner's Guide

1. Redshift and Blueshift

Press "Fire Photon Up." A photon launches from the planet. As it works against gravity, it loses energy, its wavelength gets longer, and its color shifts towards red. This is gravitational redshift. Now press "Fire Photon Down." As the photon falls, it gains energy, its wavelength gets shorter, and it shifts towards blue. This is gravitational blueshift.

2. Changing Parameters

Increase the Planet Mass for a stronger effect. Increase the Satellite Height to make the photon travel a larger distance through the gravity well, also increasing the effect.

Core Theory

The change in frequency is a consequence of gravitational time dilation. The formula for the ratio of received frequency (f_rec) to emitted frequency (f_em) is: f_rec / f_em = √(1 - 2GM/r_em c²) / √(1 - 2GM/r_rec c²). This effect is crucial for GPS satellites, which must constantly correct for it to remain accurate.
