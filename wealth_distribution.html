Econophysics: Wealth Distribution

A kinetic exchange model showing the emergence of inequality.

Overview

Why do societies tend to become unequal, even if everyone starts on a level playing field? Econophysics is a field that applies tools from statistical mechanics to understand economic phenomena. This simulation uses a simple kinetic exchange model, treating people like gas particles and money like energy. In each step, two random agents are chosen and they exchange a random amount of money. You will see that even with completely fair, random rules, the system does not remain equal. It spontaneously evolves into a highly unequal, exponential-like distribution, with a few very wealthy agents and a large majority of poor ones.

The Inevitability of Inequality?

This model is analogous to how energy is distributed among molecules in a gas. The resulting distribution is known as the Boltzmann-Gibbs distribution, which is an exponential decay. This suggests that in any system with random exchanges of a conserved quantity (like energy or money), an unequal, hierarchical distribution is the most probable outcome—it has the highest entropy.

The Role of Savings

The basic model is a bit too pessimistic. Real economies have mechanisms that can alter this outcome. The Savings Rate slider introduces one such mechanism. In this modified model, before an exchange, each agent sets aside a certain fraction of their wealth, which is safe from the transaction. The random exchange only happens with the remaining, non-saved portion.

Try this: Run the simulation with a savings rate of 0. You'll see the classic exponential distribution. Now, reset and run it with a savings rate of 0.5 or higher. Notice how the shape of the distribution changes. It is no longer a simple exponential. The peak moves away from zero wealth, a "middle class" emerges, and the long tail of extreme wealth is shortened. This demonstrates how a simple behavioral rule—saving—can fundamentally alter the macroscopic outcome and reduce inequality.
