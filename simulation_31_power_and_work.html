Plain-Language Overview

In everyday language, "work" and "power" are often used interchangeably, but in physics, they have very different meanings. **Work** is the total amount of energy needed to accomplish a task, like lifting a heavy block. **Power** is the *rate* at which you use that energy. This simulation clearly demonstrates the difference. Two cranes lift identical blocks to the same height, so they both do the exact same amount of work. However, the crane that lifts its block faster must exert more power.

Beginner's Guide — Lifting Blocks

Step 1: What is Work?

In physics, **Work** is done when a force is used to move an object over a distance. To lift a block, a crane must exert an upward force equal to the block's weight (which depends on its mass `m` and gravity `g`) over the desired height `h`. The formula is simple:

Work = Force × Distance = (m * g) * h

The key thing to notice is that this formula doesn't care about time. Lifting a 100 kg block by 10 meters requires the same amount of work whether you do it in 5 seconds or 5 minutes.

Step 2: What is Power?

Power is the measure of how fast work is done. If you do the same amount of work in less time, you are more powerful. The formula for average power is:

Power = Work / time

The unit of power is the Watt (W), which is defined as one Joule of work done per second.

Step 3: The Simulation

In this simulation, you can set the mass and height for two identical lifting tasks. You can also set the time each crane will take.

  1. Notice that the "Work Done" will be the same for both cranes, because `m`, `g`, and `h` are the same.
  2. Set `Time for Crane 2` to be smaller than `Time for Crane 1`.
  3. Click the "Lift!" button.
  4. Observe that Crane 2 moves faster and that its calculated "Power Exerted" is much higher than Crane 1's.

Analogy: Climbing Stairs

Think about climbing a flight of stairs.

  • Work: The amount of work your body does to lift your weight to the next floor is fixed. It depends only on your mass and the height of the staircase. This is the same whether you walk or run.
  • Power: If you walk up slowly, your power output is low. If you sprint up the stairs, you are doing the same amount of work in a fraction of the time. Your power output is enormous, which is why you feel much more tired and out of breath after running up!

Work Done Against Gravity

To lift an object of mass `m` at a constant velocity, one must apply an upward force `F` equal in magnitude to the downward force of gravity, `F_g = mg`, where `g` is the acceleration due to gravity (approximately 9.8 m/s²). The work done `W` in lifting the object a vertical distance `h` is:

W = F * h = m * g * h

The unit of work is the Joule (J).

Power

Power `P` is the rate at which work is done. If an amount of work `W` is done over a time interval `t`, the average power is:

P = W / t

The unit of power is the Watt (W), which is equivalent to one Joule per second (J/s).

Example: Let's lift a 1000 kg block a height of 50 m.
The work done is `W = 1000 kg * 9.8 m/s² * 50 m = 490,000 J` (or 490 kJ). This is the same for both cranes.

  • If Crane 1 takes 10 s, its power is `P₁ = 490,000 J / 10 s = 49,000 W` (or 49 kW).
  • If Crane 2 takes 5 s, its power is `P₂ = 490,000 J / 5 s = 98,000 W` (or 98 kW).
Crane 2 does the same work but requires twice the power.
