The Geodynamo

A conceptual animation of how the Earth generates its magnetic field.

Overview

The Earth behaves like a giant bar magnet, with a magnetic field that protects us from harmful solar radiation. But where does this field come from? It's not a permanent magnet. Instead, it's continuously generated by a process called the geodynamo. This simulation provides a conceptual animation of the key ingredients: heat from the solid inner core drives convection in the molten iron outer core, and the Earth's rotation (the Coriolis effect) twists this motion into helical flows. These flows of conductive material act like a giant electromagnet, generating and sustaining the planet's magnetic field.

The Three Ingredients for a Dynamo

  1. A Conductive Fluid: The Earth's outer core is made of molten iron, which is an excellent electrical conductor.
  2. Convection: Heat flowing from the solidifying inner core into the cooler mantle causes the liquid iron to churn in massive convection cells. Hot, less dense iron rises, and cooler, denser iron sinks. In the simulation, this is the basic up-and-down motion of the fluid.
  3. Rotation: The Earth's spin introduces the Coriolis effect, which deflects the moving iron. Instead of just moving up and down, the convection currents are twisted into helical, or corkscrew-like, columns. This is the crucial step.

These organized helical flows act like current-carrying wires wrapped around the Earth's axis. By the laws of electromagnetism, an electric current creates a magnetic field. The geodynamo is a "self-exciting" dynamo: the motion of the conductor through an initial weak magnetic field generates currents that, in turn, produce a magnetic field that is strong enough to sustain itself and the currents that create it. When you press "Start Dynamo," you'll see these helical flows develop and the large-scale dipole field emerge.

Magnetic Reversals

The geodynamo is not perfectly stable. Over geological time, the complex, chaotic fluid motions in the core can cause the magnetic field to weaken, become disorganized, and then re-establish itself with the opposite polarity (North becomes South and vice versa). These magnetic reversals have happened many times in Earth's history. Press the "Trigger Reversal" button to see a conceptual animation of this chaotic process.
