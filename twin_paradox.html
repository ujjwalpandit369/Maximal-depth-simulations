The Twin Paradox

An interactive narrative explaining one of relativity's most famous puzzles.

Plain-Language Overview

Imagine two twins: Terra stays on Earth, while Stella travels in a spaceship to a distant star and back at nearly the speed of light. According to time dilation, Terra would see Stella's clock running slow, so she expects Stella to be younger upon her return. But from Stella's perspective, the Earth was moving away and back, so shouldn't Terra be the younger one? This apparent contradiction is the "paradox." This simulation will show you the journey, track both twins' clocks, and visualize their paths on a spacetime diagram to reveal the resolution.

Beginner's Guide

1. The Apparent Paradox

From Terra's view, Stella is moving, so Stella's clock should be slower. From Stella's view, Terra is moving, so Terra's clock should be slower. How can both be true? This suggests a contradiction.

2. Breaking the Symmetry: Acceleration

The resolution is that the situation is not symmetrical. Terra stays in one non-accelerating reference frame. Stella must accelerate, turn around, and decelerate. This breaks the symmetry. The definitive result is that the traveling twin, Stella, will have aged less. Press "Start Trip" to see this.

3. The Spacetime Diagram: A Geometric View

The spacetime diagram on the right shows this beautifully. Terra's path through spacetime (her "worldline") is a straight vertical line. Stella's worldline is a bent, triangular path. In the strange geometry of spacetime, a straight line is the path of longest proper time. Because Terra's path is straight and Stella's is bent, Terra experiences more time.

FAQ & Troubleshooting

Is it the acceleration that causes the time difference?
Not directly. The time difference accumulates during the constant-velocity parts of the journey. The acceleration is what is *required* for the traveling twin to switch reference frames and return, breaking the symmetry.
Is this a real effect?
Yes. The Hafele-Keating experiment flew atomic clocks on airplanes. When they returned, their clocks showed time differences that matched the predictions of relativity.
