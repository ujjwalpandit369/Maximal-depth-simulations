Nuclear Cross-Sections

Visualizing the energy-dependent probability of a nuclear reaction.

Overview

How likely is it that a particle fired at an atomic nucleus will actually hit it and cause a reaction? In nuclear physics, this probability is described by a quantity called the cross-section (σ). It can be thought of as the effective "target area" the nucleus presents to the incoming particle. This area is not fixed; it depends strongly on the energy of the incoming particle. At certain "resonance" energies, the cross-section can become enormous, far larger than the physical size of the nucleus. This simulation visualizes how the cross-section changes with energy and how that affects the number of reactions in a particle beam experiment.
