Guide: The View from a Microscope

Fractal Dimensions

We are used to integer dimensions: a line is 1D, a square is 2D. Fractals are complex shapes that can have fractional, or non-integer, dimensions. A famous way to measure this is "box-counting": cover the shape with boxes of size `s` and count how many you need, `N(s)`. As you make the boxes smaller, `N(s)` grows like `(1/s)ᴰ`, where `D` is the dimension. On a log-log plot, this gives a straight line with slope `D`.

The Assouad Dimension: A Local View

The Assouad dimension is a more powerful, "local" way of thinking about dimension. It asks: "If I look at a piece of the set inside a ball of radius `R`, how many smaller balls of radius `r` does it take to cover just that piece?" This is like looking at the set through a microscope with magnification `R/r`.

The defining property is that the number of covering balls `N(R,r)` must be bounded by `C * (R/r)ᵃ` for some exponent `α`, no matter where you place your viewing window or what scales `R` and `r` you choose. The smallest `α` that works for all possible views is the Assouad dimension. It captures the "most complex" part of the fractal at any scale.
