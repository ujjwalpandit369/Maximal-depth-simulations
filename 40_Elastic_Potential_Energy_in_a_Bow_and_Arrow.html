Plain-Language Overview

When an archer draws a bow, they are doing work and storing energy in the bent limbs of the bow. This stored energy is called **elastic potential energy**. When the archer releases the string, the bow springs back to its original shape, and all that stored energy is rapidly transferred to the arrow, launching it forward as **kinetic energy**, the energy of motion. This simulation lets you explore this energy transformation. You can adjust the bow's stiffness, the arrow's mass, and how far the string is drawn back. A dynamic bar chart will show you exactly how the potential energy stored in the bow is converted into the kinetic energy of the arrow, and how these factors determine the arrow's final launch speed.

Beginner's Guide: Storing and Releasing Energy

Step 1: Stored Energy (Potential Energy)

Potential energy is energy that is stored and held in readiness. A stretched rubber band, a compressed spring, and a drawn bow all contain **elastic potential energy**. The amount of energy stored depends on how much the object is stretched or compressed, and on how "stiff" the object is. In the simulation, you can control two things that affect the stored energy:

  • Draw Distance (x): How far back you pull the string. Pulling it back farther stores much more energy.
  • Bow Stiffness (k): A measure of how hard it is to draw the bow. A stiffer bow stores more energy for the same draw distance.
When the bow is drawn, you will see the blue "Potential Energy" bar in the chart is full.

Step 2: Energy of Motion (Kinetic Energy)

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because it is moving. It depends on the object's mass and its speed. When you click **"Release"**, the bow's stored potential energy is converted into the arrow's kinetic energy.

Step 3: The Energy Transformation

Click "Release" and watch the bar chart closely. As the bowstring moves forward, the blue "Potential Energy" bar will shrink, while the green "Kinetic Energy" bar will grow. Assuming a perfect, 100% efficient bow, all of the potential energy will have been converted into kinetic energy just as the arrow leaves the string. The total amount of energy remains the same throughout the process, demonstrating the conservation of energy.

Step 4: What Determines the Arrow's Speed?

The final speed of the arrow depends on how the energy is shared.

  • A **stiffer bow** or a **longer draw distance** stores more potential energy, resulting in more kinetic energy and a higher launch speed.
  • A **heavier arrow** (larger mass) has more inertia. For the same amount of kinetic energy, a heavier arrow will have a lower final speed than a lighter arrow. You can test this with the **Arrow Mass** slider.

Core Theory: Energy Conservation

1. Elastic Potential Energy (PEe)

The energy stored in an ideal spring-like object (our bow) when it is displaced by a distance x from its equilibrium position is given by:

PEe = ½kx²

Where k is the spring constant (or "stiffness"). Notice that the stored energy increases with the square of the draw distance, making it a very powerful factor.

2. Kinetic Energy (KE)

The energy of the moving arrow is:

KE = ½mv²

3. Conservation of Energy

Assuming no energy is lost to heat or sound, the potential energy stored in the drawn bow is completely converted into the kinetic energy of the arrow as it leaves the string.

PEe, initial = KEfinal
