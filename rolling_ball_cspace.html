Configuration Space for a Rolling Ball

Visualizing the 5D configuration space of a ball rolling on a 2D plane.

Recommended device: Desktop

Plain-Language Overview

A ball's "configuration" is its position (where it is) plus its orientation (how it's rotated). Its position on a table needs two numbers (x, y), but its 3D rotation needs another three! This puts the ball's state in a 5-dimensional "Configuration Space" (C-Space). This simulation shows the ball rolling in the real world ("Workspace") and its corresponding path in a 3D "slice" of its true 5D C-Space. You'll see how a simple straight roll creates a beautiful helical (corkscrew) path in this higher-dimensional abstract space.

Beginner's Guide

1. Workspace vs. Configuration Space

Workspace (Left Panel): This is the familiar world where the ball rolls on a tabletop. We only care about its position (x, y) here. Configuration Space (Right Panel): This is an abstract space where every point represents one unique configuration (position AND orientation) of the ball. A path in the workspace corresponds to a path in C-space.

2. The "No-Slip" Condition

A rolling ball doesn't slip. This links its movement to its rotation. The distance traveled equals the ball's radius times the angle it has rotated: distance = radius × angle. The ball can't move without rotating.

3. Visualizing 5D Space

We can't draw 5D space. The right panel shows a 3D slice: the X and Y axes are the ball's position, and the vertical Z-axis is one of its rotation angles. When the ball rolls straight (along the X-axis in the workspace), its position and rotation angle both increase steadily. This creates a helix in the C-space view. It makes the hidden dimensions of motion visible!

Core Theory

The configuration of a rigid body in 3D space is an element of the special Euclidean group SE(3), represented as a pair q = (p, R), where p is the position vector and R is a rotation matrix. For a ball on a plane, this gives a 5-dimensional C-space (2 for position, 3 for orientation). The "no-slip" rolling constraint v = r (ω × ẑ) connects the linear velocity v to the angular velocity ω, defining the valid paths within this C-space. The linear relationship between distance and rotation angle, θ = distance / radius, is what generates the helical path in our C-space projection.
